Personal Diaries

Selected papers of Eleanor Ashcombe

PAGE 01 / 08
14 October 1911

The Beginning

He says the Dahlia must disappear.

I asked why a thing so precious should be hidden when it could make us both exceedingly wealthy.

He only smiled.

There are five around the one. I have marked them as he instructed.

If anyone should find the Dahlia again, they must first understand the six marks.

I fear I have agreed to something I do not entirely understand.

— E.A.
PAGE 02 / 08
16 October 1911

After the Auction

The house has been unusually quiet since the auction.

Arthur has sent no further explanation. I am told the catalogue will show the lot as withdrawn.

It is strange to see such a valuable object disappear from a catalogue as though it had never been offered at all.

He says that records are easier to alter than memories.

— E.A.
PAGE 03 / 08
18 October 1911

The Cards

He came to the house again tonight.

Before leaving, he placed the cards upon the billiards table.

Five face upward.

One remains beneath the cloth.

He told me that the order mattered more than the cards themselves.

I asked him what name I should record. He laughed.

— E.A.
PAGE 04 / 08
Undated · Private Family Note

On the Order of the Four Suits

Father always insisted that the suits be remembered in their proper order when the family played.

♦ Diamonds First
♣ Clubs Second
♥ Hearts Third
♠ Spades Fourth

He said the order mattered more than the cards themselves.

I never understood why.

— E.A.
PAGE 05 / 08
20 October 1911

The Five Hands

He spoke again of the jewel returning through five hands.

Five owners, perhaps. Five places. Five records.

I cannot tell whether he means people or merely the passage of time.

Yet he seemed certain that the five would be enough to make the story believable.

I wonder what becomes of the sixth.

— E.A.
PAGE 06 / 08
21 October 1911

The Truth

I understand now why he insisted upon the story of a theft.

The jewel was never stolen.

Arthur was instructed to mark Lot 45 as withdrawn.

The Dahlia was removed before the auction ended.

It was to disappear from the records, not from the world.

He said that one day the jewel would return through five hands.

Five appearances. Five cards. And then the sixth.

He called it a game.

— E.A.
PAGE 07 / 08
Undated · Private Note

The Sixth Hand

I remember the billiards room most clearly.

It was there that the cards were always laid out after supper.

Five would be dealt.

The sixth was always kept aside.

If the game is ever completed, the final card must return to the room where it began.

Perhaps that is what he meant when he said the sixth hand would finish the game.

— E.A.

Archival Note

Final Instruction

Archival Instruction

Eleanor's notes describe the room, the sixth card, and his hand.

The remaining note contains no further explanation.

The handwriting ends at the bottom of the page.

— E.A.
